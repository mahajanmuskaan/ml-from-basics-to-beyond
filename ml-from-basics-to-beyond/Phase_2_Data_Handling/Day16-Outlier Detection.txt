📘 Phase 2 – Day 16: Outlier Detection & Handling

In real-world data, not all values behave normally.
Some values are extremely high or low compared to the rest — these are called outliers.

❓ What Is an Outlier?

An outlier is a data point that significantly differs from other observations in a dataset.

Examples:

Extremely high cashback amount

Unusually low or zero spending

Abnormal transaction behavior

📌 Outliers may occur due to:

Data entry mistakes

Measurement errors

Rare but valid events

❓ Why Are Outliers Important in ML?

Outliers matter because they can:

1️⃣ Distort data statistics

Affect mean and standard deviation

2️⃣ Mislead ML models

Models may learn wrong patterns

3️⃣ Reduce model performance

Especially for distance-based models

4️⃣ Reveal important insights

Fraud, rare behavior, or special cases

📌 Key idea:
👉 Not all outliers are wrong — but all must be examined.

🔍 Methods Used for Outlier Detection
1️⃣ Z-Score Method

Measures how far a value is from the mean

Expressed in terms of standard deviations

📌 Common rule:

|Z-score| > 3 → potential outlier

👉 Best when data is approximately normally distributed.

2️⃣ IQR (Interquartile Range) Method

Uses the middle 50% of data

Detects values far below or above normal range

Outlier range:

Below: Q1 − 1.5 × IQR

Above: Q3 + 1.5 × IQR

👉 Very effective for skewed data.

3️⃣ Visualization (Intuition Builder)

Box plots

Histograms

Scatter plots

📌 Visual methods often reveal outliers faster than numbers.

⚙️ How Can Outliers Be Handled?

There is no single correct answer. Common approaches include:

1️⃣ Removal

If outliers are clear errors

2️⃣ Transformation

Log or scaling transformations

3️⃣ Imputation

Replace with mean or median

4️⃣ Robust Models

Use models less sensitive to outliers
(e.g., Decision Trees, Random Forests)

📌 Important rule:
👉 Always understand why an outlier exists before removing it.
