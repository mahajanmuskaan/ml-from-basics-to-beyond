# Day 25 — How Does Linear Regression Actually Find the "Best" Line?

---

## First, What Are We Even Trying to Do?

Imagine you have data points scattered on a graph — say, house sizes on the X-axis and house prices on the Y-axis. You want to draw **one straight line** that best represents the relationship between them.

But there are infinite possible lines you could draw. So the real question is:

> **What makes one line better than another? And how does the algorithm find it?**

That's exactly what today is about.

---

## The Intuition — What is "Error"?

Draw any line through your data. For each data point, measure the **vertical distance** between the actual point and your line.

```
Actual Price:    ₹50 Lakhs
Line Prediction: ₹45 Lakhs
Error:           ₹5 Lakhs  ← your line was wrong by this much
```

This distance is called the **residual** — how far off your prediction was from reality.

A good line has **small residuals**. A bad line has **large residuals**.

Simple enough. But here's the problem — some errors will be positive (predicted too low), some negative (predicted too high). If you just add them up, they cancel each other out and give you a misleadingly small total error.

**Solution:** Square each error before adding. Squaring does two things — it makes all values positive, and it **punishes large errors more heavily** than small ones.
