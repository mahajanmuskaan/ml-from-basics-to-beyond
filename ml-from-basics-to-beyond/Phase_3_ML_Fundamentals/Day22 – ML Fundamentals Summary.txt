📘 Day 22 – ML Fundamentals Summary

Today we wrap up the core thinking behind Machine Learning models.

Before algorithms, you must understand these 4 pillars:

1️⃣ What Is a Model?

A model is a mathematical system that learns patterns from data and uses those patterns to make predictions on new data. For example, if you give a model past house data (size, location, price), it learns the relationship between features and price. Later, when you input a new house’s details, it predicts the price. The goal of a model is not to memorize old data, but to make good predictions on unseen data.

2️⃣ Training vs Testing

Training is the process where the model learns from known data. Testing is when we check how well it performs on new, unseen data. For example, if you are preparing for an exam, your study material is like training data, and the final exam is like test data. If you only check performance on study material, you might think you are perfect—but the real test is how you perform in the actual exam. That’s why we separate training and testing.

3️⃣ Overfitting & Underfitting

Underfitting happens when a model is too simple and cannot capture the real pattern in data. It performs poorly even on training data. For example, predicting every house price as ₹50 lakhs regardless of size or location would be underfitting.

Overfitting happens when a model becomes too complex and memorizes the training data (including noise). It performs extremely well on training data but poorly on new data. For example, a student who memorizes exact previous exam answers may fail when questions are slightly changed. The goal is to find a balance where the model learns real patterns but does not memorize noise.

4️⃣ Bias–Variance Tradeoff

Bias refers to error due to overly simple assumptions in the model. High bias leads to underfitting. Variance refers to how much the model’s predictions change when the training data changes. High variance leads to overfitting. For example, a very simple model predicting house price only based on size may miss important factors (high bias). A very complex model considering every tiny detail may react too much to small data changes (high variance). A good model maintains a balance between bias and variance to achieve strong performance on unseen data.
